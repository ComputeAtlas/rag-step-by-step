# Fernhill Winter Riding Guide

Edition October 2025. For the Ridge 500, Metro 300 and Cargo 700. Cold weather affects an electric bike more than a
normal bicycle, because a lithium-ion battery delivers less energy when it is cold. Plan for it and the bike will
work all winter.

## How much range you lose

The table shows the share of the normal range that you can expect at different temperatures, on the same flat route in Eco.
Take the claimed range for your model from its owner's manual and multiply.

| Temperature | Share of normal range |
|---|---|
| 20 °C | 100% |
| 10 °C | 90% |
| 0 °C | 75% |
| -10 °C | 60% |

Example: a Ridge 500 that does 110 km in Eco at 20 °C should manage roughly 82 km at 0 °C. Wind, wet roads, and
soft tyres take off more. In winter a rider on the Metro 300 who normally gets 80 km should plan for about 60 km.

The battery warms itself a little as it works, so range recovers during a long ride. A battery that starts the day cold
loses the most.

## Looking after the battery in winter

1. **Keep it warm before you ride.** Store the bike in a garage if you can, and take the battery indoors the night before.
   A battery that starts at room temperature can lose noticeably less range than one that spent the night outdoors.
2. **Charge at room temperature.** The charging temperature range is 5 to 40 °C. Never charge a frozen battery. If the
   battery has been outside in freezing weather, leave it indoors for at least an hour before plugging in the charger.
3. **Riding limits.** The bike works from -10 to 40 °C. Below -10 °C the battery cannot deliver power and the display shows
   E07 (battery too cold); warm the battery indoors for an hour.
4. **Do not use Battery care mode if you need every kilometre.** The 80% limit (firmware v2.5 and later) is useful in
   summer, but in winter your range is already shorter.
5. **Charging takes a little longer.** A cool battery charges more slowly until it warms up.

Firmware v2.5 improved cold starts on the Cargo 700 below 0 °C. If your Cargo 700 hesitates when starting from cold,
check that you have v2.5 or later.

## Tyres

Air pressure falls by roughly 0.1 bar for every 10 °C drop in temperature. A tyre pumped to 3.5 bar in a warm kitchen can
be near 3.3 bar outdoors in winter. Check the pressure more often, and add a little. Do not reduce pressure for grip on ice
unless you ride a studded tyre; Fernhill does not sell studded tyres.

| Model | Pressure |
|---|---|
| Ridge 500 | 1.4 to 2.0 bar off-road, 2.2 to 2.5 bar road |
| Metro 300 | 3.0 to 4.0 bar |
| Cargo 700 | 2.5 bar unloaded, 3.2 bar loaded |

## Salt, wet and the drivetrain

- Road salt attacks metal. After a salty ride, wipe the frame, chain and brake rotors with a damp cloth. Do not use a
  pressure washer.
- Lubricate the chain more often in winter: every 100 km on the Ridge 500 and Cargo 700, and every 200 km on the Metro 300,
  rather than the usual 150 km and 300 km.
- Wet grit wears brake pads quickly. Check them at the normal interval, or sooner after a muddy winter. Ridge 500 pads
  are checked every 500 km, Metro 300 every 750 km and Cargo 700 every 400 km.
- Dry the battery contacts with a cloth after a wet ride.
- Keep the charging port cover (FH-2330-A) in place.

## Staying safe

- Braking distances are longer on wet and icy roads, particularly on a loaded Cargo 700, which needs about 40% longer to stop
  when fully loaded.
- Use the lights. The Metro 300 and Cargo 700 have lights powered by the main battery. The Ridge 500 has an accessory port
  for a light of up to 6 W (FH-8103-A).
- Avoid ice. A motor can spin a wheel on a slippery surface. Use Eco on slick roads for gentler help.
- Dress for the wind chill, because a faster ride feels colder.

## Storing the bike for the season

If you will stop riding for the winter, follow the storage steps in the battery safety document: 40 to 60% charge, indoors, at
10 to 20 °C, and a check every three months. Inflate the tyres and store the bike off the ground if you can. Book a full
service in spring.

## Winter commuter checklist

| Before you leave | Check |
|---|---|
| Battery | Charged and warm from being indoors; Battery care mode off if you need the range |
| Tyres | Pressure checked with a gauge, not by pressing with a thumb |
| Lights | Working, and the rear light is clean |
| Brakes | Dry the discs by pulling the levers gently for the first few hundred metres |
| Clothing | Gloves that still let you reach the brake levers and the buttons on the remote pad |
| Route | A slightly longer route on roads that are gritted is safer than a short one on ice |

Plan to arrive with a quarter of the battery left, because the range figure on the display falls faster in the cold than it does on a summer day.
