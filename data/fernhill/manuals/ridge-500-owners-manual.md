# Fernhill Ridge 500: Owner's Manual

Edition 4, December 2025. Covers Ridge 500 bikes with serial numbers starting `FH-R5-`. Firmware described: v2.5.
Keep this manual with your bike's receipt. If you sell the bike, pass the manual on with it.

The Ridge 500 is Fernhill's mountain e-bike: a hardtail with a mid-drive motor, a 130 mm suspension fork and a
frame-integrated battery. The number in the name is the battery size in watt-hours, rounded down to the nearest
hundred (the real figure is 504 Wh).

### Estimated range by assist level

The claimed range depends on how much help the motor gives. These figures were measured on flat tarmac with a 75 kg
rider at 20 °C. Hills, wind, cold weather, soft tyres and heavy loads reduce them.

| Assist level | Motor help | Range |

## 2. First ride

1. Fit the handlebars, stem and pedals as shown on the quick-start card (see the Ridge 500 quick-start guide). The left pedal has a left-hand thread.
2. Check that the wheels are tight, the brakes grip, and the tyres are pumped to the pressure in section 5.
3. Charge the battery fully before the first ride.
4. Press the power button on the display. Hold the walk-assist button for two seconds to confirm the motor responds.
5. Start in Eco on a flat, quiet road. The motor only helps when you pedal.

Register your bike within 30 days of buying it at fernhillbikes.example/register. You will need the serial number and
your order number. The serial number is stamped under the frame, next to the pedals, and starts with `FH-R5-`. The
registration benefit depends on when you bought the bike; see the warranty policy for your year.

## 3. The display and controls

The Dash 2 display has a colour screen and a remote pad with three buttons (plus, minus, mode) and a separate
walk-assist button.

- **Plus / minus:** change assist level.
- **Mode (short press):** cycles trip distance, total distance, range left and cadence.
- **Mode (hold 2 seconds):** opens the menu. Menu items: Battery (charge percentage, health, cycle count), Display
  (brightness, units), Bike (service reminder, firmware version) and Light accessory port.
- **Walk assist (hold):** the bike moves along at up to 6 km/h while you hold the button and walk beside it.
- **Auto-off:** the display switches itself off after 10 minutes without use.

The cycle count under Menu > Battery shows how many full charge cycles the battery has had. One cycle means the
battery has delivered a total amount of energy equal to its full capacity, so two top-ups of 50% count as one cycle.

## 4. Charging and battery care

### Charging

1. Switch the bike off.
2. Plug the charger into the wall socket first, then connect it to the charging port under the rubber cover on the
   down tube. The charger light is red while charging and green when full.
3. You can also remove the battery with its key and charge it indoors. Press the lock cylinder, turn the key and slide
   the pack out.
4. Do not leave a charging battery unattended overnight on a carpet, bed or sofa.

Use only the charger supplied with your bike (part FH-2310-B) or an identical replacement bought from Fernhill. Any
other charger can damage the battery and is not covered by the warranty. Chargers of part number FH-2310-A made
between March and June 2025 (date code 0325 to 0625) were part of a safety recall; check the label on yours and read
the safety notice.

### Looking after the battery

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Daily use | Charge when convenient. Partial charges are fine. |
| Not riding for more than 2 weeks | Store at 40 to 60% charge, indoors, at 10 to 20 °C |
| Storage for months | Check the charge every 3 months and top up to 50 to 60% |
| Cold weather | Keep the battery indoors until you ride. Range falls by roughly a quarter near 0 °C |
| After a wet ride | Dry the battery contacts with a cloth before refitting |
| Leaving the battery empty | Never. A battery left flat for weeks may not charge again (error E09) |

If the battery is dented, swells, smells sweet or gets hot while idle, stop using it, move it outside onto a
non-flammable surface and contact Fernhill support. Batteries must never go in household waste; return them to any
Fernhill dealer for recycling.

Fernhill's Battery care mode (80% charge limit) was added in firmware v2.5. Switch it on under Menu > Battery. It
helps the battery last longer if you rarely need the full range.

Use a torque wrench. Overtightening carbon seatposts or aluminium bars causes cracks.

| Fastener | Torque |
|---|---|
| Handlebar clamp | 5 Nm |
| Stem steerer bolts | 5 Nm |
| Seatpost clamp | 6 Nm |
| Crank arm bolts | 50 Nm |
| Pedals | 35 Nm |
| Rear thru-axle | 12 Nm |
| Brake rotor bolts | 4 Nm |
| Motor mounting bolts (dealer only) | 18 Nm |

### Maintenance schedule

| Task | When |
|---|---|
| Check tyre pressure | Before every ride |
| Check brakes, quick releases and bolts | Before every ride |
| Lubricate the chain | Every 150 km, or after a wet ride |
| First check of bolts and spokes | After the first 100 km or 4 weeks, whichever comes first |
| Check brake pads (replace at 1.5 mm of pad left) | Every 500 km |
| Check chain wear (replace at 0.5% stretch) | Every 1,000 km |
| Clean battery contacts | Every 6 months |
| Service the suspension fork | Every 100 riding hours |
| Replace brake fluid | Every 2 years |
| Full service at a Fernhill dealer (includes battery health test and firmware check) | Every 2,000 km or 12 months, whichever comes first |

Wear parts: brake pads for this bike are part FH-3105-A and the chain is FH-7101-A. The full parts list is in the
parts catalogue.

### Cleaning

Wipe the frame with a damp cloth and mild soap. Do not use a pressure washer or hose on the motor, battery port or
display. Water forced past the seals is not covered by the warranty.

## 6. Display error codes

When the controller detects a fault the display shows the code E and two digits. Switch the bike off, wait ten
seconds and switch it on again; many faults clear. If the code returns, follow the fix in the table. Codes are
specific to this model. Other Fernhill models use some of the same numbers for different faults.

| Code | Meaning on the Ridge 500 | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | Battery not detected: no communication with the battery pack | Switch off, remove and refit the battery until it clicks. Wipe the contacts with a dry cloth. If it returns, see a dealer. |
| E02 | Motor not responding | Check the motor cable plug at the bottom bracket is pushed fully home. |
| E03 | Torque sensor signal out of range | Switch off. Restart without putting any weight on the pedals. |
| E04 | Speed sensor: no signal | Check the magnet on the rear wheel. The gap to the sensor must be 3 to 5 mm. |
| E05 | Walk-assist button pressed at switch-on | Release the button, restart. Clean the button if it sticks. |
| E06 | Battery over-temperature (above 60 °C) | Stop and park in shade for 30 minutes. |
| E07 | Battery too cold (below -10 °C) | Warm the battery indoors for an hour. |
| E08 | Motor over-temperature (above 85 °C) | Stop and wait 20 minutes. Use a lower assist level on long climbs. |
| E09 | Battery voltage too low | Connect the charger. A deeply discharged pack can take 30 minutes to start charging. |
| E10 | Charger fault: charger voltage outside range | Use the FH-2310-B charger supplied with the bike. |
| E11 | Brake cut-off switch active at switch-on | Release both brake levers and restart. Check the lever return. |
| E12 | Shift-assist sensor fault | Switch off and on. If it returns, have the sensor checked. The bike rides normally. |
| E13 | Accessory port overload (more than 6 W drawn) | Unplug the light or accessory and restart. |
| E14 | Display and controller firmware do not match | Update both to the same version in the Fernhill Service app or at a dealer. |
| E15 | Controller over-current | Switch off, wait 10 seconds, restart. Avoid starting in a very high gear on a steep slope. |
| E16 | Battery cells out of balance | Leave the battery on the charger for 12 hours. |
| E17 | Battery calibration drift (added in firmware v2.4): range estimate unreliable | Ride the battery down below 10%, then charge to 100% without interruption. |
| E18 | Battery not locked fully | Turn the key to lock the battery, then restart. |
| E19 | Motor phase fault | Do not ride. Contact a dealer. |
| E20 | Display memory fault | The display needs replacing (part FH-5102-A). |

## 7. Firmware

The Ridge 500 ships with the latest firmware and can be updated with the Fernhill Service app (free for phones). An
update takes about 12 minutes and needs the battery above 50%. Display and controller always update together and must
carry the same version number. Release notes are published for every version. Your firmware version is shown under
Menu > Bike. Firmware v2.3 or later is needed to use the newer B battery pack (FH-2291-B) on a Ridge 500 built before 2025;
see the parts catalogue and release notes for the details.

## 9. Safety

- Wear a helmet. Read the brake section before riding downhill.
- Do not modify the motor or controller. Tuning devices void the warranty and make the bike illegal on public roads.
- The motor cuts out at 25 km/h on public roads in the UK, Ireland and the EU. This is the law, not a fault.
- Do not ride through water deeper than the bottom of the motor.
- Disc brakes get hot. Do not touch the rotors after a long descent.
- Switch off before fitting accessories or lifting the bike.

## 10. Riding off-road with a motor

A mid-drive motor sends its power through the chain, so how you shift matters.

- Shift before a climb, not during it. Under full pedal load, shifting wears the chain and cassette quickly. The
  shift-assist sensor briefly cuts the motor while you change gear, but it works best if you ease off the pedals.
- Use Eco or Tour for long days. Boost gives the shortest range (up to 45 km) and heats the motor on long climbs.
- In loose ground keep a steady cadence of 70 to 90 revolutions per minute. The torque sensor reads how hard you
  push, so smooth pedalling gives smoother help.
- Before a big descent, check the brake pads. Four-piston brakes stop well, but worn pads fade when hot.
- After a muddy ride, brush off the dirt before it dries, wipe the chain and refit the battery cover.
- Rinse nothing from above the motor. Use a bucket and sponge, never a jet of water.

## 11. Transport and storage

- The bike weighs 23.8 kg with the battery and 20.9 kg without it. Remove the battery before lifting it onto a car
  rack. Take the battery with you, not in the boot, if the car will stand in direct sun.
- Do not transport a damaged battery. Airlines and ferries have their own rules for lithium-ion batteries; ask before
  you travel. Fernhill support can give you a battery data sheet on request.
- Keep the bike indoors, dry and out of freezing conditions when you can. A garage is fine; a damp shed is not.
- If the bike will not be used for a season, follow the storage line in the battery table, inflate the tyres and hang
  or stand the bike so that the tyres do not take the weight.

## 12. Problems without an error code

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Range much lower than the table | Cold, soft tyres, high assist level, hills | Check the tyres, use Eco, keep the battery warm. If it is still low, ask for a battery health test. |
| Motor feels weak | Low charge (the bike reduces help under 10%) | Charge the battery. |
| Squealing brakes | Wet or contaminated pads | Clean the rotors with brake cleaner, bed in the pads with 10 firm stops. |
| Clicking from the cranks | Loose crank bolts or dry chain | Check the crank bolts at 50 Nm and lubricate the chain. |
| Display will not switch on | Battery empty or not seated | Check the battery lock and charge level. |
| Display flickers at dusk (firmware v2.5) | Automatic brightness | Set Menu > Display > Brightness to Manual. |

## 13. Regulatory information

- The Ridge 500 is an electrically power-assisted cycle. Its FH-M85 mid-drive motor has a nominal continuous rated power of 250 W and
  stops giving assistance at 25 km/h, or sooner if you stop pedalling. This means that it is treated as a normal bicycle in
  the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands and Germany.
- The bike is designed to meet the European standard for electrically power-assisted cycles, EN 15194. The declaration of
  conformity is available at fernhillbikes.example/doc.
- The sound pressure level of the motor at the rider's ear is below 70 dB(A).
- The vibration at the handlebars and saddle is below the level that requires a warning.
- The serial number (starting `FH-R5-`) is stamped under the frame, next to the pedals. Keep a note of it.
- The manufacturer is Fernhill Bikes Ltd, Hollinwick.

## 16. Questions owners ask about the Ridge 500

**How far will I really go?** It depends on the assist level. On flat tarmac with a 75 kg rider at 20 °C the manual's figures are 110 km
in Eco, 80 km in Tour, 60 km in Sport and 45 km in Boost. A hilly trail ride in Sport will give nearer 40 to 50 km.

**Why does the motor stop helping at 25 km/h?** The law in the UK, Ireland and the EU requires it for a bike that is treated as a
normal bicycle. The Ridge 500 will freewheel faster than that downhill, but the motor does not push.

**Can I ride in the rain and through puddles?** Yes in the rain and through shallow puddles. The warranty does not cover damage from
riding through water deeper than the bottom of the motor.

**Which brake pads do I need?** FH-3105-A for the Ridge 500. The Cargo 700 uses a different compound (FH-3105-B).

**Which battery do I need?** Bikes built from 2025 use FH-2291-B. Earlier bikes use FH-2291-A, or FH-2291-B if the firmware is v2.3
or later.

**How do I find my firmware version?** Hold Mode for two seconds, open Menu > Bike. It shows the display and controller builds.

**What does the walk-assist button do?** Hold it and the bike moves along at up to 6 km/h. Release it and the bike stops within a fraction of a second.

**Can I fit a rack or mudguards?** Mudguards (FH-8301-A) fit. The frame has no rack mounts.

**The suspension fork feels harsh. What should I do?** Set the fork pressure to suit your weight using the fork's own guide, then check it every
100 riding hours when the fork is serviced.

## 17. How the motor assistance works

The Ridge 500 does not have a throttle. It helps only when you pedal, and it decides how much to help by measuring how hard you push.
A torque sensor inside the FH-M85 motor measures the force on the cranks many times a second. The controller (the small computer in
the frame) multiplies that force by the number for your assist level: 40% in Eco, 80% in Tour, 140% in Sport and 220% in Boost. If you push with 100
watts of effort in Boost, the motor adds about 220 watts. The motor's rated power is 250 W nominal, and its peak torque is 85 Nm, which is what
lets it climb steep trails.

Three things limit the help. First, the legal limit: at 25 km/h the controller fades the help out over a couple of km/h. Second, the battery:
the less charge is left, the more the controller protects the cells, and below 10% the bike reduces its help. Third, temperature: if the
motor passes 85 °C the bike shows E08 and stops helping until it cools down; if the battery passes 60 °C it shows E06.

Because the motor is a mid-drive, it turns the chain and the gears together with you. That is why the Ridge 500 climbs well in a low gear, and
why it is kinder to the chain to shift before the climb rather than during it. The shift-assist sensor cuts the motor for a moment while you
change gear to protect the drivetrain; if that sensor stops working, the display shows E12 and the bike carries on normally.

Inside the battery is a battery management system (BMS). It watches each group of cells for voltage, current and temperature, and talks to the controller. When the
BMS cannot be heard, the display shows E01. The newer B pack, FH-2291-B, uses a newer language for that conversation, which is why older bikes
need firmware v2.3 or later to understand it.
