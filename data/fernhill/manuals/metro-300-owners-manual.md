# Fernhill Metro 300: Owner's Manual

Edition 4, December 2025. Covers Metro 300 bikes with serial numbers starting `FH-M3-`. Firmware described: v2.5.
Keep this manual with your bike's receipt.

The Metro 300 is Fernhill's city e-bike: a light step-through frame, a quiet rear hub motor, a seven-speed hub gear
that needs almost no maintenance, and built-in lights. The number in the name is the battery size in watt-hours,
rounded down to the nearest hundred (the real figure is 324 Wh).

## 2. First ride

1. Fit the handlebars and pedals following the quick-start guide. The left pedal has a left-hand thread.
2. Raise the saddle until your leg is almost straight with the pedal at its lowest. The step-through frame lets you
   mount from either side.
3. Charge the battery fully.
4. Press the power button on the remote pad. The Dash 1 display shows the assist level and battery bars.
5. Pedal. The hub motor helps once the wheel turns. The lights switch on with the long press of the plus button.

Register your bike at fernhillbikes.example/register within 30 days of purchase. You will need the order number and the
serial number, which is stamped under the frame, next to the pedals, and starts with `FH-M3-`. What registration gives you depends on the year
you bought the bike; check the warranty policy for that year.

## 3. The display and controls

The Dash 1 display is a small monochrome screen with a remote pad of three buttons: plus, minus and mode. There is no
separate walk-assist button; hold the minus button for two seconds to start walk assist.

- **Plus / minus:** change assist level.
- **Plus (hold 2 seconds):** lights on or off.
- **Mode (short press):** cycles trip distance, total distance and range left.
- **Mode (hold 2 seconds):** opens the menu: Battery (charge, health, cycle count), Units, Bike (service reminder,
  firmware version).
- **Auto-off:** the display switches itself off after 10 minutes without use.

The cycle count under Menu > Battery shows the battery's full charge cycles. A cycle is counted when the battery has
delivered energy equal to its full capacity in total, so two 50% top-ups make one cycle.

## 4. Charging and battery care

### Charging

1. Switch the bike off.
2. Plug the charger into the wall, then into the charging port on the down tube, under the rubber cover.
3. The charger light is red while charging and green when full.
4. You may also remove the battery with its key and charge it indoors.

Use only the charger supplied with your bike (FH-2310-B) or an identical replacement bought from Fernhill. Do not use
a charger from another bike or brand. Chargers of part number FH-2310-A made between March and June 2025 (date code
0325 to 0625), supplied with Metro 300 and Ridge 500 bikes, were part of a safety recall. Look at the label on your
charger and read the safety notice if it matches.

### Looking after the battery

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Daily use | Charge when convenient. Partial charges are fine. |
| Not riding for more than 2 weeks | Store at 40 to 60% charge, indoors, at 10 to 20 °C |
| Storage for months | Check every 3 months and top up to 50 to 60% |
| Cold weather | Keep the battery indoors until you ride. Range falls by roughly a quarter near 0 °C |
| Leaving the battery empty | Never. A flat battery may not charge again (error E09) |

Battery care mode (an 80% charge limit) arrived in firmware v2.5 and is switched on under Menu > Battery.

If the battery is damaged, hot while idle or smells of chemicals, stop using it and contact support. Never throw
batteries in household waste.

## 5. Maintenance

The Metro 300 is designed for people who want a bike to simply work. The hub gear sits in a sealed case, and the chain
is shorter and cleaner than on a derailleur bike.

| Maximum on the sidewall | 4.5 bar |

| Fastener | Torque |

| Task | When |

Wear parts: brake pads for this bike are part FH-3110-A and the chain is FH-7102-A. See the parts catalogue for
the full list.

## 6. Display error codes

When the controller detects a fault the display shows E and two digits. Switch the bike off, wait ten seconds and
switch it on again. If the code returns, use the table. These codes are specific to the Metro 300. Other Fernhill
models use some of the same numbers for different faults.

| Code | Meaning on the Metro 300 | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | Battery not detected: no communication with the battery pack | Remove and refit the battery until it clicks. Wipe the contacts with a dry cloth. If it returns, see a dealer. |
| E02 | Motor not responding | Check the hub motor cable plug near the rear dropout is pushed fully home. |
| E03 | Cadence sensor signal missing | Check the sensor disc by the pedals is not loose or rubbing. Have a dealer check it if the code returns. |
| E04 | Hub motor speed signal missing | The speed sensor is inside the motor. Cannot be fixed at home: see a dealer. |
| E05 | Remote pad button stuck at switch-on | Release all buttons, restart. Clean around the buttons if they stick. |
| E06 | Battery over-temperature (above 60 °C) | Stop and park in shade for 30 minutes. |
| E07 | Battery too cold (below -10 °C) | Warm the battery indoors for an hour. |
| E08 | Motor over-temperature (above 90 °C) | Stop and wait 20 minutes. |
| E09 | Battery voltage too low | Connect the charger. A deeply discharged pack can take 30 minutes to start charging. |
| E10 | Charger fault: charger voltage outside range | Use the FH-2310-B charger supplied with the bike. |
| E11 | Brake cut-off switch active at switch-on | Release both levers and restart. Check the levers return fully. |
| E12 | Not used on this model | - |
| E13 | Front or rear light circuit short | Check the light wiring and plugs for damage. Switch the lights off. |
| E14 | Display and controller firmware do not match | Update both to the same version in the Fernhill Service app or at a dealer. |
| E15 | Controller over-current | Switch off, wait 10 seconds, restart. |
| E16 | Not used on this model | - |
| E17 | Battery calibration drift (added in firmware v2.4): range estimate unreliable | Ride the battery down below 10%, then charge to 100% without interruption. |
| E18 | Battery not locked fully | Turn the key to lock the battery, then restart. |
| E19 | Motor phase fault | Do not ride. Contact a dealer. |
| E20 | Display memory fault | The display needs replacing (part FH-5101-A). |

## 7. Firmware

Update with the Fernhill Service app (free for phones). The update takes about 12 minutes and needs the battery above
50%. Display and controller update together and must carry the same version number (otherwise E14 appears). Your
version is shown under Menu > Bike. Firmware v2.3 or later is needed to use the newer B battery pack (FH-2219-B) on a
Metro 300 built before 2025.

## 9. Safety

- Wear a helmet and use the lights after dark.
- Do not tune the motor or controller. It voids the warranty and makes the bike illegal on public roads.
- Assistance ends at 25 km/h by law, not by fault.
- Do not ride through water deeper than the bottom of the motor.
- Disc brakes get hot; do not touch the rotors straight after a long descent.

## 10. City riding and security

- Lock the frame and a wheel to something fixed, with a lock rated for e-bikes. The battery is lockable but can be
  taken with you if you leave the bike for long.
- Park out of the rain where you can. Rain is fine; standing in a puddle for days is not.
- Hub motors give a smooth, quiet ride, but they help less on very steep hills than a mid-drive motor does, since
  the peak torque is 40 Nm. In hilly cities use Tour or Sport and pick a lower gear before the climb.
- Lights stay on at a low level for a few minutes after you switch off, so you are not left in the dark at a junction.
- Check the tyres weekly. A puncture-resistant tyre resists glass and thorns but still loses air slowly.

## 11. Problems without an error code

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Range much lower than the table | Cold, soft tyres, high assist level | Check the tyres, use Eco, keep the battery warm; ask for a battery health test if it stays low. |
| Rattle from the rear | Loose carrier bolts | Tighten the carrier bolts to 9 Nm. |
| Hub gear skips | Cable stretch | Book the first service; the cable is adjusted there. |
| Lights do not work | Loose plug or E13 | Check the plugs; see E13. |
| Lights flicker with the motor | Loose battery | Refit the battery and lock it. |
| Squealing brakes | Wet or dirty pads | Clean the rotors and bed in the pads with 10 firm stops. |

## 12. Regulatory information

- The Metro 300 is an electrically power-assisted cycle. Its FH-H35 rear hub motor has a nominal continuous rated power of 250 W and
  stops giving assistance at 25 km/h, or sooner if you stop pedalling. This means that it is treated as a normal bicycle in
  the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands and Germany.
- The bike is designed to meet the European standard for electrically power-assisted cycles, EN 15194. The declaration of
  conformity is available at fernhillbikes.example/doc.
- The sound pressure level of the motor at the rider's ear is below 70 dB(A).
- The vibration at the handlebars and saddle is below the level that requires a warning.
- The serial number (starting `FH-M3-`) is stamped under the frame, next to the pedals. Keep a note of it.
- The manufacturer is Fernhill Bikes Ltd, Hollinwick.

## 13. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Assist level | How much extra power the motor adds to your pedalling |
| Cadence | How fast you turn the pedals, in revolutions per minute |
| Charge cycle | The use of energy equal to the full battery's capacity in total |
| Controller | The electronic unit that decides how much power the motor gives |
| Display | The screen and buttons on the handlebars (the Dash 1 on this bike) |
| Firmware | The software inside the display and the controller |
| Nm (newton metres) | A measure of twisting force. This bike's motor peaks at 40 Nm |
| Sensor | On this bike a cadence sensor by the pedals detects that you are pedalling |
| Walk assist | Gentle motor help while you push the bike beside you |
| Wh (watt-hours) | The energy stored in the battery |

A short note on the motor type: a hub motor sits inside the rear wheel and pushes the wheel directly, which is quiet and needs little upkeep.

## 13. Questions owners ask about the Metro 300

**How far will I really go?** On flat tarmac with a 75 kg rider at 20 °C: 80 km in Eco, 60 km in Tour and 45 km in Sport. Commuting uphill
in Sport can give 35 km.

**Does the Metro 300 have lights?** Yes, a front and rear LED light powered by the main battery. Hold the plus button for two seconds to turn them on or off.

**How do I start walk assist?** Hold the minus button for two seconds. The bike then moves at up to 6 km/h.

**Why is there no E12 or E16 on my display?** Those codes are not used on the Metro 300. It has a hub gear (no shift sensor) and its
battery does not report cell balance.

**Which brake pads do I need?** FH-3110-A for the Metro 300 (2-piston calipers).

**Which battery do I need?** Bikes built from 2025 use FH-2219-B. Earlier bikes use FH-2219-A, or FH-2219-B if the firmware is v2.3 or later.

**The range estimate jumped to 100% after I refitted the battery.** That was a known issue in firmware before v2.4. Update the bike.

**Does the Metro 300 need a lot of maintenance?** No. The hub gear is sealed, the chain stays cleaner, and the first service at 100 km or 4
weeks adjusts the gear cable. After that, service every 2,500 km or 12 months.

## 14. How the motor assistance works

The Metro 300 does not have a throttle. It helps only when you pedal. A cadence sensor by the pedals notices that the cranks are turning, and the
controller then tells the rear hub motor to add power in proportion to the assist level you chose: 40% in Eco, 80% in Tour and 140% in Sport. Unlike
the Ridge 500 and Cargo 700, the Metro 300 does not measure how hard you push; it measures that you are pedalling, which is why its help feels
smooth and steady rather than responding to every push. That is also why error E03 on this bike is about the cadence sensor and not a torque sensor.

The FH-H35 motor sits in the rear wheel hub. It is rated at 250 W nominal, with a peak torque of 40 Nm. Having the motor in the wheel keeps the
chain and the seven-speed hub gear free of the motor's force, which is part of why the Metro 300 needs so little maintenance. The hub motor also
contains its own speed sensor. If that signal is lost, the display shows E04, and it cannot be repaired at home.

At 25 km/h the controller fades the help out. If the battery falls below 10% it reduces the help to make the charge last. If the motor passes
90 °C the bike shows E08 and rests until it cools. The lights are powered from the main battery, and stay on for a short time after you switch off, so you are not
left in the dark at a junction.

The battery has a management system that watches voltage, current and temperature. If it cannot be heard by the controller, the display shows E01. The newer B pack,
FH-2219-B, talks in a newer language, which is why a bike built before 2025 needs firmware v2.3 or later to use it.
