## Customer's question

Bram asked in chat: "I thought the Metro 300 does 80 km. After one day of commuting up and down hills I am at 20% after 35 km.
What am I doing wrong?"

## Conversation

**Callum:** Hi Bram. Which assist level are you in, and how heavy are you with your bag?

**Bram:** Sport all the way. I weigh about 95 kg and carry a laptop bag of 8 kg.

**Callum:** Thanks. The Metro 300 has a 324 Wh battery. The 80 km figure is for Eco on flat road with a 75 kg rider. In Sport the
manual's table gives up to 45 km on the flat. You are about 28 kg heavier than the test rider and your route is hilly,
so 35 km in Sport is what I would expect.

**Bram:** That is a pity. Can I do something short of buying a bigger battery?

**Callum:** Yes. Switch to Tour for most of the route, and use Sport only on the steepest climbs. Shift to a lower gear
before the climb: the Metro 300's hub motor has 40 Nm peak torque, so it works best if you help it. Check the tyres too;
the Metro 300 wants 3.0 to 4.0 bar.

**Bram:** I will try Tour and report back.

**Callum:** If you ever want more range from a Fernhill bike, the Ridge 500 has a 504 Wh battery. The Metro 300 itself has
no bigger battery option.

## Resolution

No fault. Range is consistent with Sport level, a 103 kg total load (the Metro 300's maximum system weight is 130 kg) and
hills. Advice given: ride in Tour, shift down before climbs, and keep tyres at 3.0 to 4.0 bar.

## Note for the support team

Compare the customer's route and weight with the range table in the Metro 300 manual; the table assumes a 75 kg rider on flat ground.
