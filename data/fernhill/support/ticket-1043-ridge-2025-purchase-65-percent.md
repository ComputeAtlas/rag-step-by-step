## Customer's question

Aiko wrote: "I bought my Ridge 500 on 20 December 2025. The battery health is now 65% after 380 cycles. Your 2026 policy says a battery is
defective under 70%. Please replace it."

## Conversation

**Callum:** Thank you, Aiko, and I'm sorry to be the bearer of bad news. The policy that applies is the one in force on the day you bought the bike. You
bought on 20 December 2025, so the 2025 policy applies, not the 2026 one, which only covers bikes bought from 1 January 2026.

**Aiko:** What is different?

**Callum:** Under the 2025 policy a battery is defective when it holds less than 60% of its original capacity while under warranty, with cover of 2
years or 500 cycles. At 65% and 380 cycles your battery is within both limits, but above the 60% line. The 70% figure is only in the 2026 policy.

**Aiko:** That seems unfair for a purchase 12 days before the change.

**Callum:** I understand. I have no power to change which policy applies, but I can help. A Master Service dealer can run the battery
health test for free. If the dealer measures below 60%, the claim would be accepted. If it confirms 65%, the pack remains a normal one that has had a lot of use. The
replacement is FH-2291-B at £449. As a goodwill gesture, I can offer free delivery on it (normally £15).

**Aiko:** The dealer test showed 64%.

*18 September 2026.* Aiko bought the FH-2291-B at £449 with the delivery fee waived.

## Resolution

The 2025 policy applied (purchase date 20 December 2025): defective below 60%, not 70%. The battery measured 64%, so it was
not defective. Customer bought FH-2291-B for £449; delivery fee waived as a goodwill gesture.

## Note for the support team

A bike bought on 20 December 2025 is under the 2025 policy. Do not apply the 2026 70% threshold to it.
