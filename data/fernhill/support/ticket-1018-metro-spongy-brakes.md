## Customer's question

Xavier phoned: "The rear brake on my Metro 300 pulls almost to the handlebar. It still works but it feels spongy. I rode about
2,000 km this year and have never had it looked at."

## Conversation

**Rhys:** Spongy hydraulic brakes usually mean air in the line or old fluid. Your manual says brake fluid should be replaced
every 2 years, and the brakes checked every 750 km. Have the pads been checked?

**Xavier:** No, never.

**Rhys:** Then I suggest a full service. For the Metro 300 that is every 2,500 km or 12 months, whichever comes first, and
it costs £89. A brake bleed is £35 per brake if you only want that. Pads for this bike are part FH-3110-A (£19) and are
replaced when 1.5 mm is left.

**Xavier:** What if the pads are fine?

**Rhys:** Then the bleed solves it. The labour rate is £48 per hour, and a service also includes tightening bolts and a
battery health test.

*26 November 2025.* The dealer replaced worn rear pads and bled both brakes. Xavier paid for the service and the pads
as normal wear; the brakes are now firm.

## Resolution

Worn rear pads and air in the line. Dealer visit with full service, pads and bleed. Not a warranty matter: brake pads are
a wear part.

## Note for the support team

Spongy brakes: check pad thickness and bleed. Pads are wear parts; service price for a Metro 300 is £89.
