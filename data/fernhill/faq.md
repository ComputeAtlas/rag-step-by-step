# Fernhill Bikes: Frequently Asked Questions

Last updated 18 December 2025. If an answer here disagrees with a more specific document (a manual, a policy or a
firmware release note), the more specific and more recent document is right.

## Choosing a bike

### Which Fernhill bike is right for me?

- **Ridge 500:** trails, hills and fast commuting. Mid-drive motor with 85 Nm of torque, 130 mm fork, 12 gears.
- **Metro 300:** city riding and shorter commutes. Light (19.6 kg), quiet hub motor, 7-speed hub gear, built-in lights.
- **Cargo 700:** shopping, children and work loads. Carries up to 200 kg in total, with an 80 kg rack.

### How much do they cost?

From 1 January 2026 the prices, including VAT, are £3,299 for the Ridge 500, £1,899 for the Metro 300 and £4,499 for the
Cargo 700. Delivery to mainland UK addresses is free. You can also pay monthly for the Cargo 700 if you are a business
buying three or more (see the Cargo 700 business plan).

### Why are the model numbers 500, 300 and 700?

The number is the battery capacity in watt-hours, rounded down to the nearest hundred. The Ridge 500 has a 504 Wh battery,
the Metro 300 a 324 Wh battery and the Cargo 700 a 720 Wh battery.

### Do I need a licence or insurance?

In the UK, Ireland and the EU, a bike with a 250 W motor that stops helping at 25 km/h is treated as a normal bicycle and needs
no licence. All three Fernhill bikes are built to that rule. Insurance is not required by law, but we suggest you cover
the bike against theft. Fernhill does not sell insurance.

### Can I ride a Fernhill bike in the rain?

Yes. The bikes are built for splash and rain (rating IPX4). Do not use a pressure washer, do not submerge the bike and do not
ride through water deeper than the bottom of the motor. Water damage from deep water is not covered by the warranty.

## Range and charging

### How far will my bike go on a charge?

The best-case figures are up to 110 km (Ridge 500, Eco), up to 80 km (Metro 300, Eco) and up to 100 km unloaded or 70 km
fully loaded (Cargo 700, Eco). These were measured on flat tarmac with a 75 kg rider at 20 °C. Real life is shorter:
hills, wind, cold, soft tyres and high assist levels all take distance off. Near 0 °C expect about a quarter less.

### How long does charging take?

With the supplied charger: Ridge 500, 5 hours from empty to full; Metro 300, 3 hours 30 minutes; Cargo 700, 4 hours
30 minutes. To 80%: 3 h 30 min, 2 h 30 min and 3 h respectively.

### Can I use a different charger?

Use the charger supplied with your bike, or an identical replacement bought from Fernhill with the same part number. The
Ridge 500 and Metro 300 use FH-2310-B (36 V, 3 A). The Cargo 700 uses FH-2320-A (48 V, 4 A). Chargers from other makers can
damage the battery and are not covered by the warranty. A 36 V charger plugged into a Cargo 700 shows error E10.

### Is my charger part of the safety recall?

Only FH-2310-A chargers with date codes 0325 to 0625 (March to June 2025) are affected. Check the label on your charger.
Affected chargers are replaced free. See safety notice SN-2025-03.

### What is a charge cycle?

One full charge cycle means the battery has delivered a total amount of energy equal to its full capacity. Two top-ups of
50% count as one cycle, not two. The count is shown under Menu > Battery > Cycles.

### How do I look after the battery?

Charge when you like; partial charges are fine. If you will not ride for more than two weeks, store the battery at 40
to 60% charge, indoors, at 10 to 20 °C. Never leave it empty for weeks. Since firmware v2.5 you can switch on Battery care
mode, which stops charging at 80%.

### Can I take my battery on a plane?

No. All three Fernhill batteries are above 160 Wh, so none can be carried on a passenger flight.

## Warranty

### How long is the warranty?

It depends on the year you bought the bike. For bikes bought on or after 1 January 2026:

- Frame: lifetime, for the original owner.
- Motor and controller: 2 years.
- Battery: 3 years or 800 full charge cycles, whichever comes first.
- Display, lights and wiring: 2 years.
- Paint and finish: 1 year.

If you register your bike within 30 days of buying it, the battery warranty grows to 4 years (the 800-cycle limit stays).

Bikes bought in 2025 are under the 2025 policy, which is different: a 5-year frame warranty and a battery warranty of
2 years or 500 cycles. Read the policy for the year on your receipt.

### How do I register my bike?

Go to fernhillbikes.example/register and enter your serial number and order number within 30 days of the purchase. The
serial number is stamped under the frame, next to the pedals, and starts with `FH-`. Registration also lets us tell you
about safety notices.

### What is not covered?

Wear parts (tyres, inner tubes, brake pads, chains, cassettes and grips), crash damage, misuse, riding through deep
water, batteries charged with the wrong charger, and bikes used for rental or delivery work. The one exception, for bikes
bought from 1 January 2026, is a Cargo 700 bought on a Fernhill business plan.

### How do I make a claim?

Write to warranty@fernhillbikes.example with your order number, serial number and a photo. For the battery, include the
cycle count shown in the display. We reply within 2 working days (bikes bought from 1 January 2026) and fix approved claims within
10 working days.

### When is a battery defective?

For bikes bought from 1 January 2026, when it holds less than 70% of its original capacity while still under warranty.
Under the 2025 policy the level is 60%. A Fernhill dealer can test the battery in about 15 minutes.

## Firmware and display

### What is the latest firmware?

The latest version is v2.5, released on 28 August 2025. You can install it with the Fernhill Service app.

### How do I update the firmware?

Open the Fernhill Service app, connect to the bike, keep the battery above 50% and follow the steps. The display and the
controller are updated together and take about 12 minutes. A mismatch between them shows error E14.

### How long before the display switches itself off?

After 10 minutes without use.

### What does an error code mean?

Each model's owner's manual has the full table. The codes are not identical across models: for example E03 means a
torque sensor fault on the Ridge 500 and Cargo 700 but a missing cadence sensor signal on the Metro 300, and E12 is not used on
the Metro 300. Many codes clear if you switch the bike off, wait ten seconds and switch it on again.

### Which firmware do I need for the new battery?

The new B battery packs (FH-2291-B for the Ridge 500 and FH-2219-B for the Metro 300) need firmware v2.3 or later on bikes
built before 2025. Bikes built from 2025 already have it.

### Where do I find the part number I need?

In the parts catalogue, which lists every part by number with the models it fits and the price. If you are not sure, tell
us your serial number and we will confirm the part.

### How often should I service the bike?

Ridge 500: every 2,000 km or 12 months. Metro 300: every 2,500 km or 12 months. Cargo 700: every 1,500 km or 6 months.
The first check is free after the first 100 km or 4 weeks.

### How much is a service?

£109 for the Ridge 500, £89 for the Metro 300 and £139 for the Cargo 700. The labour rate is £48 per hour. Warranty
repairs are free.

### Can I fix the bike myself?

Yes for tyres, tubes, pads, chains and most small parts. We suggest a dealer for motors, controllers and brake bleeding.

## Riding and maintenance

### Is the motor allowed to help me above 25 km/h?

No. All three bikes stop the assistance at 25 km/h (15.5 mph), as the law in the UK, Ireland and the EU requires. You can pedal
faster than that; the motor just stops helping. Tuning the motor voids the warranty and makes the bike illegal on the road.

### What is walk assist?

A feature that moves the bike along at walking pace while you push beside it. On the Ridge 500 and the Metro 300 it runs
at up to 6 km/h, and on the Cargo 700 at up to 4 km/h. On the Ridge 500 and Cargo 700 you hold the walk-assist button. On the
Metro 300 you hold the minus button for two seconds.

### How do I switch the lights on?

The Metro 300 and Cargo 700 have lights powered by the main battery. On the Metro 300 hold the plus button for two seconds. The
Ridge 500 has no lights fitted as standard; it has an accessory port for a light of up to 6 W.

### What tyre pressure should I use?

Ridge 500: 1.4 to 2.0 bar off-road and 2.2 to 2.5 bar on the road. Metro 300: 3.0 to 4.0 bar. Cargo 700: 2.5 bar unloaded and
3.2 bar loaded. The maximum printed on each sidewall is 2.5 bar, 4.5 bar and 3.5 bar respectively.

### How often should I lubricate the chain?

Every 150 km on the Ridge 500 and the Cargo 700, and every 300 km on the Metro 300, or after any wet ride.

### Can I wash the bike with a hose?

A garden hose is not recommended either. Wipe the frame with a damp cloth and a bucket of soapy water. Never use a
pressure washer on the motor, the battery port or the display. Water forced past the seals is not covered by the warranty.

### Can I fit a rack or a child seat?

The Cargo 700 comes with a rack rated for 80 kg and can take up to two child seats. The Ridge 500 has no rack mounts. Child seats
should be fitted by a dealer.

## Troubleshooting

### My bike will not switch on.

Check that the battery is locked in place and charged. Press and hold the power button for one second. If the display stays blank
and the battery is charged, leave the bike connected to the charger for 30 minutes (a deeply discharged pack can take that long to
start) and try again.

### The motor does not help even though the display is on.

Check that you are in an assist level above zero, that no brake lever is pulled and that the wheel magnet or sensor is in place. If
a code appears, look it up in the manual.

### Why does the range estimate change during the ride?

It is a prediction based on how you rode recently. Hills, headwinds and the assist level change it. On the Cargo 700, set the Load
menu (Empty, Half or Full) so the estimate counts the weight you carry.

### The display shows E14. What do I do?

The display and controller have different firmware versions. Update both to the same version with the Fernhill Service app. It
happens after a display or controller has been replaced.

### Which error codes are most likely to be a real fault?

E19 (motor phase fault) and E20 (display memory fault) always need a dealer. E19 means do not ride the bike. Others, such as E03,
E05 and E11, are often caused by touching the pedals or brake levers at switch-on, and clear after a restart.

## Service centres and dealers

### Where can I get my bike serviced?

At any Fernhill service centre (Brackenford and Wexcombe), Fernhill store (Hollinwick, Stonehallow and Tarnmouth) that offers
service, or at an authorised dealer at Service or Master Service level. See the store locations document.

### What is the difference between the dealer levels?

Sales dealers sell bikes and parts and do the free first check. Service dealers also service bikes and update firmware. Master
Service dealers also replace motors, controllers and displays and do warranty repairs.

### Can I get my bike collected from home?

Within 30 miles of a Fernhill service centre we collect and return it for £39 each way.

### What if I live outside the UK?

Fernhill bikes ship to Ireland, the Netherlands and Germany. Dealers in those countries can service them.
